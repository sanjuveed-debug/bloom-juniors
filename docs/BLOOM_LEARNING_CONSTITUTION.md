# Bloom Learning Constitution

## Purpose

Bloom Juniors helps children build strong foundations so they can understand new
ideas, ask better questions, and keep learning with confidence.

## What Every Bloom Experience Must Do

1. **Begin with wonder.** Start from a real question a child may ask, not a fact
   chosen only because it appears on a syllabus.
2. **Build the foundation first.** Explain the smallest useful idea before adding
   names, rules, dates, or complexity.
3. **Let the child think.** Ask for a prediction, choice, observation, or
   explanation before revealing the answer.
4. **Make the idea visible.** Use a concrete example, story, experiment, movement,
   image, or model. Interaction must serve understanding.
5. **Use honest language.** Keep explanations age-appropriate without making them
   scientifically, historically, or culturally false.
6. **Connect knowledge.** Show how science, language, maths, history, place,
   culture, values, and everyday life illuminate one another.
7. **Respect roots and differences.** Teach culture, faith, and heritage with
   warmth, accurate sourcing, and room for families to hold different beliefs.
8. **Return learning to real life.** End with something a child can notice, try,
   ask, draw, build, or discuss away from the screen.
9. **Show growth without pressure.** Preserve discoveries and reflections.
   Rewards may celebrate effort, but scores must not replace curiosity.
10. **Invite the grown-up in.** Offer one optional, specific conversation prompt
    that helps the family continue the idea together.

## Bloom Learning Formats

Bloom does not force every subject into the same activity. The format must fit
the kind of understanding being built.

- **Inquiry:** Wonder -> Predict -> Observe -> Explore -> Explain -> Connect.
- **Story:** Enter -> Listen -> Notice -> Discuss -> Retell -> Connect.
- **Practice:** Recall -> Model -> Try -> Feedback -> Apply -> Reflect.
- **Project:** Imagine -> Plan -> Make -> Test -> Improve -> Share.
- **Conversation:** Question -> Perspective -> Reason -> Listen -> Reconsider.

Wonder Why uses the inquiry format. It is one doorway into the curriculum, not
the curriculum itself.

## Quality Bar

A lesson is ready only when a child can answer three questions:

1. What did I notice?
2. Why does it happen?
3. Where can I see or use this idea in my life?

The experience should be short enough to finish while curiosity is alive and
rich enough that the child wants to look again.
