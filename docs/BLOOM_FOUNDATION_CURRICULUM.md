# Bloom Foundation Curriculum

## The Direction

Bloom Juniors is not a collection of school exercises or disconnected answers
to children's questions. It is a connected foundation for understanding.

The child should gradually learn:

- how to communicate, count, reason, and investigate;
- how living and physical systems work;
- how people, places, and societies change over time;
- where their family and cultural roots sit within a diverse world;
- how beliefs, values, evidence, and perspectives differ;
- how to care for themselves, relate to others, make things, and contribute.

Phonics and maths remain essential. They are tools that unlock the wider
foundation rather than the entire purpose of the app.

## Five Foundation Strands

### 1. Thinking and Communication

Language, reading, writing, number sense, mathematical reasoning, logic,
questions, evidence, memory, explanation, and creative expression.

**Existing strength:** Sound Pop, Story Room, Number World, Shape World, logic,
and the junior English and maths modules.

**Needed:** more explanation, representation, argument, investigation, and
cross-subject application.

### 2. The Living and Physical World

The body, living things, habitats, materials, forces, movement, energy, light,
sound, weather, Earth, and space.

**Existing strength:** broad topic coverage through Curious Science, Anatomy,
and Planet World.

**Needed:** fewer isolated facts and more models, prediction, observation,
simple experiments, evidence, and age progression.

### 3. People, Place, and Time

Home, community, maps, environment, geography, chronology, sources, historical
change, migration, work, trade, invention, and civic life.

**Existing strength:** countries, landmarks, maps, and some history moments.

**Needed:** a local-to-global journey, real chronology, evidence from objects
and sources, multiple causes, ordinary people's lives, and connected history
rather than trivia.

### 4. Roots, Culture, and Meaning

Family heritage, language, arts, customs, sacred stories, religions,
worldviews, values, celebrations, identity, and respectful difference.

**Existing strength:** Sacred Stories and creative activities establish a base.

**Needed:** family-selected roots, living culture beyond festivals, reviewed
tradition-specific language, distinctions between belief/history/science, and
space for children to compare without ranking.

### 5. Self, Relationships, and Agency

Feelings, attention, health, movement, safety, friendship, responsibility,
money, practical judgment, creativity, service, and contribution.

**Existing strength:** movement, body, shop, and piggy-bank experiences.

**Needed:** emotional literacy, digital and physical safety, conflict repair,
responsibility, practical projects, and opportunities to improve real life.

## Age Progression

### Ages 3-4: Notice and Name

Start with the child's body, family, home, senses, objects, movements, songs,
and familiar stories. Children name, match, imitate, sequence, and compare.
Learning is audio-first, concrete, short, and shared with an adult where useful.

### Ages 5-6: Patterns and Causes

Children make predictions, identify patterns, describe simple causes, order
events, use maps and timelines, explain choices, and connect lessons to daily
life. Models remain visible and concrete.

### Ages 7-9: Systems and Evidence

Children connect systems, use evidence, consider scale, compare sources and
perspectives, recognise uncertainty, explain multiple causes, and apply ideas
through projects.

The same big idea returns with greater depth. A three-year-old notices a shadow;
a six-year-old connects shadows to a light source; a nine-year-old uses a model
of Earth's rotation and evaluates where that model is limited.

## Eleven Connected Arcs

1. **Me and My Mind**
2. **Family, Roots, and Belonging**
3. **Language, Stories, and Ideas**
4. **Number, Pattern, and Measure**
5. **Living Things and Interdependence**
6. **Matter, Force, Light, and Sound**
7. **Earth, Sky, and Deep Time**
8. **From Home to the World**
9. **How Life Changed**
10. **Belief, Values, and Traditions**
11. **Making, Serving, and Creating**

These are not menu categories that a child must scroll through. They are the
curriculum underneath daily recommendations, stories, projects, practice, and
family prompts.

## Culture, Faith, and Roots

This area requires more care than simply adding stories about gods or
festivals.

1. Parents identify relevant family traditions, languages, places, and stories.
2. Bloom distinguishes between:
   - what a tradition teaches or believes;
   - what historical sources can establish;
   - what scientific investigation explains.
3. Content names the tradition whose perspective is being described.
4. Similarities can create empathy, but differences are not flattened.
5. Cultures appear as living family and community experiences, not only ancient
   history, food, clothing, or festivals.
6. Sacred content receives source review and, where possible, review from
   knowledgeable members of that tradition.
7. Parents can preview and guide this content without turning differences into
   a warning or stigma.

## Experience Formats

- **Inquiry:** for science and observable questions.
- **Story:** for language, history, faith, values, and perspective.
- **Practice:** for phonics, fluency, number, spelling, and retrieval.
- **Project:** for design, art, service, investigation, and application.
- **Conversation:** for identity, ethics, evidence, and multiple perspectives.

A strong weekly path uses several formats. It does not turn every idea into a
quiz, game, or "Why?" card.

## What Existing Bloom Content Becomes

- **Sound Pop, Story Room, Number World:** foundational tools.
- **Curious Science and Planet World:** reorganised into progressive system
  models and investigations.
- **World GK:** evolves from country facts toward place, people, chronology,
  sources, and connected history.
- **Sacred Stories:** becomes part of a reviewed Roots, Culture, and Meaning
  strand with family context.
- **Da Vinci, projects, movement, shop:** become ways to apply and express
  learning, not side activities.
- **Wonder Why:** remains an inquiry format within the wider spine.

## Product Rules

1. The child sees one clear next experience, not eleven curriculum categories.
2. The parent sees the broader foundation and evidence of development.
3. Recommendations balance strands over time instead of repeating the easiest
   or most-played module.
4. Every saved learning record identifies strand, arc, big idea, age outcome,
   format, and real-world connection.
5. New content must close a documented curriculum gap or deepen an existing
   progression. A standalone fact is not enough.

## Build Order

### Phase 1: Establish the Spine

- Encode strands, arcs, age outcomes, and cultural guardrails.
- Tag existing modules and lessons against the spine.
- Add parent roots and interests settings with careful defaults.

### Phase 2: Recompose Existing Content

- Turn broad science facts into progressive investigations.
- Turn geography/history facts into local-to-global timelines and stories.
- Review Sacred Stories for source, tradition, age, and perspective.
- Connect practice modules to meaningful weekly arcs.

### Phase 3: Fill the Largest Gaps

- Family, Roots, and Belonging.
- How Life Changed.
- Matter, Force, Light, and Sound.
- Self, Relationships, and Agency.
- Making, Serving, and Creating projects.

### Phase 4: Make Growth Visible

- Parent view by foundation strand and big idea.
- Child portfolio of discoveries, stories, projects, and explanations.
- Recommendations that balance depth, fluency, curiosity, and family context.

## Success Test

Bloom succeeds when a child can increasingly say:

1. **I noticed something.**
2. **I can explain the foundation underneath it.**
3. **I know how it connects to other things.**
4. **I can ask a better next question.**
5. **I can use what I learned in my life.**
